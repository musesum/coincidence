Coincidence

A New Social Currency

Based on:

  • who you've met,
  • what you were doing,
  • when and where you've been,
  • how it was verified and
  • why it matters.

Each member is sovereign:

  • they own their own data
  • they opt-in to any community
  • they can remain private to new communities
  • while building reputation through presence

We propose a research project that combines:

  • Farcaster as the social graph
  • BitChat as the transport layer of BLE, NFC, UWB
  • Personal beacons broadcasting tokens
  • Community certificates to bind trust
  • Crypto binding of tokens and certificates

Working draft, November 2026. HereCaster is the first application. Nothing here is a product guarantee or an offer of any token.

HereCaster

Scan or Opt-in?

Today, for each event, someone puts up a QR code, you take out your phone, take a picture, and claim an NFT. Over and over and over again. Or maybe, you were offline, or the presenter moved to the next screen. Oh well.

Instead, you opt-in to a community you trust, and over the course of the next few days, or weeks, you simply show up. You can be online or offline, it doesn't matter. Your phone does the rest. Frictionless.

HereCaster

One connection between two phones

Each phone advertises a short handle. When two meet, they exchange a fresh, signed record that names both handles and the time window.

The signed exchange runs on iOS and on Android. A signature shows which key signed. It does not show who held the phone or how far away it was.

An example

One ride, six silent questions

Consider a shuttle ride: Ayan arrives at 14:00, and Mira rides with him from pickup A to an apartment at address B. We label this trip T14 and its shuttle S1. A current member credential, yesterday's driver record and today's timestamp could all be authentic while describing different rides.

The question joins who, what, when, where, how, and optional why. HereCaster connects records to one event and discloses enough for a verifier to check that connection. Why can be implicit: the apartment address suggests that Mira intends to go to the apartment.

A proposed trial. The trip labels and the eligibility rule are illustrative.

HereCaster

Time, in epochs

Handles rotate every 600 seconds, so a listener cannot follow one device for long. A meeting is a set of epochs in which two phones found each other.

Proposed: a daily hash of the records in a public log, so a record can later show it existed that day.

The Petri dish

A gathering, seen from your phone

You are at the centre. Rings mark true distance, each ten times farther out, which the phone cannot read. What it has are boundaries of different kinds. Pick one.

The Johnson limit

How different can two logs be and still be one room?

Each phone logs the handles it heard in an epoch. Two phones in one room hold nearly the same log. Treat your log of s handles as a room record with t repair symbols.

With up to t/2 wrong sightings, rounded down, a log repairs to exactly one room. Up to the Johnson limit, s − √s (s − t − 1), it repairs to a short list, and an earlier commitment picks the right one. Past the limit there is no repair.

For 15 handles and 10 repair symbols that is 5 wrong sightings, then 7.

What it is not

A distance. It measures agreement between logs. It is not part of the working design: missed sightings only lower a count, and the limit gives honest and dishonest logs the same allowance.

Threat mode

What an attacker brings

An attack is a mix of keys, radios, accounts, relays, people and time. Each route is shown by what it needs and what limits it. Pick a route.

The premise

Trust as a web of prior interactions

Keep the order of meetings, not only the map. The same three people, met in a different order, support different conclusions.

Trust should grow from histories that were corroborated separately. More devices in one hand add nothing. Friends who travel together remain separate people.

Each question gets one of three answers: supported, contradicted or unknown. There is no single trust score.
